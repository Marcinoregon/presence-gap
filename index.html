What is the Presence Gap?

The Gap Between Documentation and Identity

A candidate enters an interview room carrying a polished AI-generated résumé. When asked what drew them to this field, the answer sounds right — but does not hold. The document exists. The career does not yet.

This is the Presence Gap: a career-domain-specific configural condition in which externally polished career-readiness artefacts coexist with simultaneous deficits in career identity formation, developmental career relatedness, and work-purpose clarity.

The Presence Gap is distinguished from the additive co-occurrence of its component deficit states by three features: a common causal mechanism (developmental bypass via substitutive AI), a configural threshold structure, and a unique SCCT self-efficacy deprivation signature mediated by work volition.

⚙
Common Mechanism Substitutive AI developmental bypass — one cause, three simultaneous deficits
⬡
Configural Threshold All three dimensions must co-occur; sub-threshold on any one is not the construct
⚡
Efficacy Signature (SCCT) Deprivation of performance-accomplishment channels, exacerbated by low work volition

Theoretical Architecture

The Foundation of the Framework

The Presence Gap integrates four major theoretical frameworks to explain how developmental bypass impacts career entry.

Career Construction Theory (CCT)

Focus: Career Identity Formation

CCT (Savickas, 2013, 2019) holds that career identity is built through the recursive, effortful process of narrating vocational experience. Career adaptability is the psychosocial infrastructure through which this construction occurs. AI bypasses the creation of "provisional selves" (Ibarra, 1999) needed to build stable identity.

Developmental Network Theory (DNT)

Focus: Developmental Career Relatedness

Higgins and Kram (2001) establish that career-relevant support is built through differentiated engagement with multiple developmental partners (sponsors, mentors, peers). Substitutive AI use disrupts this by mediating or bypassing the relational encounters that career preparation tasks were formerly designed to initiate.

Psychology of Working Theory (PWT)

Focus: Work-Purpose Clarity

PWT (Duffy et al., 2016) focuses on decent, dignified work. Its key mechanism for the Presence Gap is work volition. When AI optimises career materials for market legibility rather than genuine values alignment, the candidate fails to develop the internalised motivation required to drive self-determined career choices.

Social Cognitive Career Theory (SCCT)

Focus: Linking Mechanism

SCCT (Lent et al., 1994; Lent & Brown, 2013) specifies that self-efficacy flows through four sources: performance accomplishments, vicarious learning, social persuasion, and physiological arousal management. Substitutive AI use deprives candidates of the two most powerful: performance accomplishments (lost when AI generates the artifact) and physiological arousal management (lost when candidates avoid unscripted prep). The configural co-occurrence is uniquely devastating because a lack of work-purpose removes the motivation needed to rebuild identity and networks.

Boundary Conditions

Three AI Use Modes

Not all AI-assisted career preparation produces developmental bypass. Three modes are theoretically distinguishable, and only one leads to the Presence Gap. See Proposition 3.

⚠ High Presence Gap Risk

Substitutive Ghostwriter

AI generates the résumé, cover letter, or interview answers. The user reviews and edits but does not substantially author or reflect. Developmental work is bypassed. This mode removes reflective, relational, and values-clarifying career preparation work. Leads to developmental non-reinvestment (saved time is offloaded, not redirected).

✓ Low Presence Gap Risk

Iterative Feedback

User drafts independently; AI critiques, challenges, and suggests revisions. Authorship is retained. However, this mode collapses toward Mode 1 if the user passively accepts AI suggestions without evaluative engagement.

✦ Developmental Enhancement

Dialogic Scaffolding

AI functions as a questioning partner—asking about experiences, values, and goals—helping the user surface content they then author independently. Developmental work is preserved and potentially enhanced because the cognitive labor of articulation remains with the user.

Equity & Culture

Equity Paradox & Cultural Boundaries

Generative AI presents unique equity opportunities and developmental risks, while the expression of the Presence Gap varies across cultural frameworks.

The Equity Paradox

Those who stand to gain the most from generative AI as a career-preparation equalizer may also bear the highest developmental costs when human support is unavailable.

For first-generation and lower-SES students, AI-assisted writing acts as an equalizer, providing access to professional self-presentation templates and the "tacit register" of the corporate market that more socially capitalized peers inherit. This access benefit is real and powerful.

The paradox is sharp: when AI is used in substitutive mode (Mode 1) without mentoring or structured career services, it bypasses the reflection, network-building, and values-testing that actually build career readiness. The appropriate response is not restriction, but equity-conscious investment in developmental recovery infrastructure.

Cultural Boundary Conditions

The developmental bypass mechanism is universal, but its expressions differ. The tripartite dimensions of the Presence Gap are culturally patterned:

Western Individuation

Assumes an individualist, self-directed model of career identity formation. Deficits manifest as a lack of personal narrative ownership and individual career volition.

East Asian Guanxi

Identity is relationally embedded in networks blending affective and instrumental ties. The deficit manifests as a failure to build organic, network-embedded career positioning.

African Ubuntu

Centered on communal becoming ("I am because we are"). The relational deficit manifests as detachment from collective career-developmental structures rather than individual sponsors.

South Asian Hierarchy

Vocational purpose is shaped by family obligation and social hierarchy. The deficit manifests as an inability to integrate personal interest with collective obligations.

Practitioner Tools

Configural Screening Protocol

A three-dimension rapid assessment designed for a 10–15 minute intake to identify likely Presence Gap cases before full assessment.

Dimension 1: Career Identity Formation

"Walk me through one of the experiences on your résumé — not the bullet point, but what actually happened and what you took from it."

Follow-up: "What would you say is distinctive about how you approach that kind of work?"

"If your résumé disappeared and you had to describe yourself to an employer in your own words, what would you say?"

Flag: Responses indicating inability to narrate own experience or dependence on AI-authored text.

Dimension 2: Developmental Career Relatedness

"Tell me about a professional — a supervisor, mentor, or colleague — who has seen your actual work or heard you talk about your goals in your own words. What did they make of it?"

Probes whether a developmental relational encounter — not merely a transactional one — has occurred.

"Has anyone in your field given you feedback that changed how you think about your direction or your strengths?"

Flag: Absence of a developmental relational encounter; all connections described transactionally.

Dimension 3: Work-Purpose Clarity

"If you had two offers at the same salary and title, what would you use to choose between them?"

Probes whether the client has an internalised values framework or defaults to external criteria.

"What drew you to this field — not the practical reasons, but what actually interests you about the work itself?"

Flag: Defaults to external criteria without values engagement.

Case Study

Meet Ana

A 23-year-old first-generation student referred after two unsuccessful final-round interviews. She presents with a polished AI-generated résumé and apparent interview anxiety.

Initial Presentation

Initial presentation suggests interview anxiety or inexperience. However, Ana engages warmly in the unscripted counselling conversation, ruling out dispositional social anxiety. The difficulty is career-context-specific.

Career Identity Probe

"Walk me through one of those experiences — not the bullet point, the moment itself."

Ana repeats the bullet point description but cannot narrate the lived experience or the decisions she made in specific terms. The artifact exists, but the career identity behind it has not yet been constructed. (CAAS concern and curiosity: lower tertile).

Relatedness Probe

"Tell me about a professional relationship that changed how you think about your work."

Ana describes both supervisors transactionally — as people who assigned tasks or provided references. She has contacts, but not a developmental network. (RHI mentor subscale: lower tertile).

Purpose Probe

"If you had two equal offers, what would you use to choose?"

Ana defaults to benefits, location, and salary, and notes that she chose the field because her professor said it was a good fit. She cannot articulate an internalized, values-grounded rationale for the work itself. (MLQ-P and DWS: lower tertile).

Configural Assessment & Differential Diagnosis

Full Presence Gap configuration confirmed. Ana's AI use was consistently substitutive, but also rational: it gave her access to a professional register that more socially capitalized peers may have inherited. The diagnosis does not pathologize the choice; it names the developmental work still required.

A higher-SES student using AI for efficiency presents with an identical configural profile and requires the same recovery work. The identification does not pathologise the choice; it names the developmental work needed.

Intervention: Mode 1 to Mode 3 Migration

The goal is not to withdraw AI but to migrate to Mode 3.

  • Identity Formation: Ana writes a handwritten free-write about one résumé experience. AI is then used in Mode 3 (scaffolding) to refine the language, ensuring the core narrative remains her own.
  • Developmental Relatedness: Maps contacts by the quality of support they provide, then plans and conducts two unscripted informational interviews.
  • Work-Purpose Clarity: Identifies a moment of genuine absorption in academic or service work to surface internal motivations and core values.

Empirical Testing & Scale Validation

Research & Psychometrics

Operationalizing the Presence Gap construct through psychometric scale development, undergraduate CURE design, and behavioral mock interviews.

P1

Substitutive AI Use Predicts Presence Gap

Substitutive AI use in career preparation will be positively associated with Presence Gap membership, controlling for overall AI-use frequency, SES, and access to career guidance.

If a well-powered study finds no mode-dependent differences in tripartite co-occurrence rates, P1 is disconfirmed.
P2

Incremental Predictive Validity over Additive Deficits

Presence Gap membership will predict early-career outcomes (job satisfaction, unscripted performance, time-to-first-mentor) above and beyond the additive effects of its three component dimensions. Subsidiary: This is mediated by Work Volition.

If individuals with the full tripartite configuration show outcome profiles equivalent to matched individuals with asymmetric deficit profiles, the configural claim is disconfirmed.
P3

Mediation and Moderation by Recovery Resources

The relationship between substitutive AI use and early-career outcomes is mediated by Presence Gap membership and moderated by access to developmental recovery resources (mentoring, counselling). Interventions shifting users from Mode 1 to Mode 3 will reduce PG indicators over 6 months.

Content Validity & Quantitative Fit Standards

To test the scale, the research team runs expert validity reviews and cross-sectional EFA/CFA quantitative fitting models.

Item-Level CVI (I-CVI)

I-CVI = A / N

Proportion of experts rating an item as "relevance 3 or 4" (quite/highly relevant). Threshold: I-CVI ≥ 0.78 (for 8 experts) is required to retain the item.

Modified Kappa (K*)

K* = (I-CVI - Pc) / (1 - Pc)

Adjusts Item CVI for chance agreement probability (Pc). A value of K* ≥ 0.75 is classified as "Excellent" and required for inclusion.

Scale CVI Average (S-CVI/Avg)

S-CVI/Avg = Σ(I-CVI) / K

Average of all item CVI values in a subscale. Threshold: S-CVI/Avg ≥ 0.90 indicates robust scale-level validity.

Aiken's V Index

V = S / [n * (c - 1)]

Validates item quality using the full ordinal rating scale (rather than dichotomizing). Retention target: V ≥ 0.75.

Quantitative Factor Analysis & Fit Cutoffs

Once surveyed (Target developmental sample N ≥ 480 completed observations), items with poor variance (SD < 1) or low item-total correlation (r < 0.30) are pruned. The psychometrics team runs promax rotated Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA), followed by Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) using Mplus, validating fit against standard cutoffs: RMSEA ≤ 0.06, TLI ≥ 0.95, CFI ≥ 0.95, and SRMR ≤ 0.08.

Course-Based Undergraduate Research Experience (CURE)

This project is modeled as a low-intensity, hybrid CURE design that combines the supportive tiered infrastructure of a traditional research lab apprenticeship (ATURE) with the collaborative and scalable classroom environment of a CURE. Anchored in Gelso's Research Training Environment (RTE) model to maximize student research self-efficacy.

1. Item Construction & Validation

Responsible for generating initial deductive theoretical items, qualitative inductive student focus group items, managing the 8-expert review panel, and calculating the content validity indices (CVI/Kappa).

2. Cognitive Interviewing & Qualitative Team

Executes Tourangeau's 4-stage processing model (Comprehension, Retrieval, Judgment, Response) across 15 peer interviews (3 sequential rounds), coding transcript behavior errors to refine wording.

3. Survey Operations & Psychometrics Team

Coordinates digital deployment, data cleaning (managing missing values with '999' labels), runs EFA and CFA fit indices, and models Latent Profile Analysis (LPA) to isolate the Presence Gap profile.

Behavioral Validation: Mock Interview Protocol

To avoid self-report bias, 80 students (40 Presence Gap Profile vs 40 Aligned Profile) participate in structured mock interviews. Evaluators are masked to their self-report profile.

Prepared / Scripted Phase

Standard questions provided 48 hours in advance (e.g. "Walk me through your résumé"). Allows substitutive users to present highly structured, pre-packaged AI-generated responses.

Unscripted Follow-Up Phase

Spontaneous, behavioral follow-ups (e.g. "Tell me about a time you led through real uncertainty, and what that actually felt like"). Bypasses rehearsed scripts to evaluate true adaptability.

Performance Drop Formula

Performance Drop = Prepared Score - Unscripted Score

Evaluated using the Mock Interview Rating Scale (MIRS). Hypothesized: The Presence Gap LPA class will show a significantly larger Performance Drop compared to the Aligned class.

Mock Interview Rating Scale (MIRS) Coding Rubric

Evaluation across 8 metrics (Anxiety, Dependability, Collaboration, Experience sharing, Vocation interest, Authenticity, Professionalism, Rapport):

Narrative Fluency & Coherence

Underperforming (1-2): Defaults to highly structured, generic, or canned language. Freezes or struggles when asked for detail.

Exemplary (5): Smoothly transitions to personalized, contextual self-narratives. Articulates specific personal lessons.

Authenticity & Self-Reflective Depth

Underperforming (1-2): Focuses solely on superficial details; cannot explain underlying motivation or values of past work.

Exemplary (5): High self-awareness. Discusses setbacks, emotional self-regulation, and internal values with honesty.

Relational Adaptability

Underperforming (1-2): Shows high anxiety when interrupted. Avoids eye contact and fails to build rapport.

Exemplary (5): Exhibits active listening, adapts tone, and naturally builds rapport with the interviewer.

Vocal Confidence & Physiological Regulation

Underperforming (1-2): Demonstrates vocal tremors, long pauses, and signs of panic (fidgeting, rapid breathing).

Exemplary (5): Modulates vocal pitch, maintains calm, deliberate breathing, and uses pause time strategically.
